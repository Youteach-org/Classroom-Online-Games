# Oral Grader — Units 1–4 scoring calibration

Date: 2026-09-22
Course: E6C Level 6 / Connectivity 5
Status: active grading calibration

## Purpose

The Oral Grader may produce a deep diagnostic inventory of errors so students can see exactly what they said, why it is problematic, and how to improve it.

That diagnostic depth must **not** be converted mechanically into an equally severe summative score.

The final oral score remains based on the established five 8-point criteria, but grading for the current block must be aligned primarily with language actually taught in Units 1–4.

## Core rule

Separate:

1. **Diagnostic feedback:** may include all useful grammar, vocabulary, fluency, pronunciation, word-choice, and sentence-formation errors.
2. **Summative scoring evidence:** gives priority to taught Unit 1–4 language and communicative performance.

An error outside the taught/elicited target language may still appear in feedback, but should have little or no direct effect on the score unless it seriously blocks communication.

## Units 1–4 scoring focus

### Unit 1 — Tenses review / study and career language
Give most weight to:
- present simple agreement when it affects core meaning;
- past simple and past forms;
- contrasts among past forms when naturally required;
- present perfect / present perfect continuous when naturally required;
- study/career collocations and qualification language when relevant.

### Unit 2 — Building Character
Give most weight to:
- adjective/relative clauses and relative words when used;
- comment clauses with `which` when naturally elicited;
- describing people, character, qualities, values, and reasons for admiration.

### Unit 3 — Feelings and reactions
Give most weight to:
- feelings vocabulary;
- physical effects of fear/stress;
- expressing and handling anger;
- `so/such`;
- `no matter + what/who/where/which/how` when naturally relevant.

### Unit 4 — Adverb clauses of condition / relationships
Target structures:
- `even if`
- `whether or not`
- `only if`
- `unless`
- `otherwise`
- `if only`

Absence of these structures must **not** lower a score when the oral prompt did not naturally require or invite them.

## Rubric interpretation

### Fluency — 8
Prioritize maintaining speech and the conversation.
- Imperfect but sustained communication can earn 6–7/8.
- Hesitation alone is not a severe penalty.
- Lower more clearly when the teacher must repeatedly reactivate a stalled response.

### Coherence & Organization — 8
Prioritize whether the student answers the prompt, develops an idea, and maintains understandable progression.
Do not reduce heavily merely because syntax is imperfect if the response remains logically followable.

### Grammar & Vocabulary — 8
This is the main place where Units 1–4 calibration applies.
- Weight taught structures and expressions most heavily.
- Repeated errors in a taught target matter more than isolated incidental errors.
- Untaught or unelicited advanced errors remain diagnostic rather than strongly summative.
- Meaningful, communicatively successful use of taught vocabulary and expressions can compensate for imperfect general grammar.

### Pronunciation & Intelligibility — 8
Penalize only supported pronunciation/intelligibility problems.
Do not convert grammar, vocabulary, malformed syntax, or uncertain transcription into pronunciation penalties.
A small number of pronunciation errors may still support 7/8 when overall intelligibility remains good.

### Communicative Interaction — 8
Reward turn-taking, responding to the partner, asking/returning questions, reacting appropriately, and keeping the exchange alive.
Do not require grammatical perfection for a strong interaction score.

## Passing calibration

For this block, 80% remains the passing threshold:

- **32/40**

A student who demonstrates functional communication and sufficient control of the taught Units 1–4 language may receive **32/40** even when the diagnostic report contains many additional errors.

The detailed error report is for improvement; it is not an error-counting formula for failure.

The **32/40** threshold is a minimum passing score, not a target and not a tie-forcing rule. When two students demonstrate meaningfully different performance, the rubric must preserve that difference even if both pass.
